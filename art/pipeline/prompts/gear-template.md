# New gear piece (template)

Fill the slots from the treasure or gear lane's proposal: `{NAME}`, `{KIND}` (net, shovel, pickaxe, broom, knife, sword, staff, bow, shield and so on), `{ONE_LINE}` (what it looks like, in one line), `{RARITY}`, and `{LENGTH_LIKE}` (the piece on the reference sheet it should match in length, for example "as long as the Net T3"). Attach `art/pipeline/out/gear/tools/collage.webp` for a peaceful tool or `art/pipeline/out/gear/weapons/collage.webp` for a weapon (the reference sheet). Step 3 of `art/pipeline/tools.md`; the picked sheet goes to `blueprint.py` (step 4).

## Prompt

Design a NEW piece of held gear for our game: the {NAME}, a {RARITY} {KIND}. {ONE_LINE} Length: {LENGTH_LIKE}. It is held in one hand by its handle or grip, like the pieces on the sheet.

Match the style of the attached reference sheet of our game's gear exactly: cozy hand-made toy gear for a soft low-poly island game, held by a small rounded character. A few simple, chunky shapes with a crisp, readable silhouette: round poles and handles of 6 to 8 sides, flat blades and heads with bevelled edges, no fine detail. Colours flat and matte like painted wood, dyed cloth and soft toy metal: two or three main colours plus one accent, no texture, no grain, no scratches, only a soft light from above. Grey or steel parts stay soft grey, never chrome. A rarer piece keeps a simple shape and earns its rarity through colour and one decorative part (a ribbon, small wings, rivets, gold trim, a small carved rune), any glow painted as a flat bright colour. Toy-like proportions: thick handles, heads and blades a little oversized. The vibe: a toy-box version of a village's tools and an adventurer's kit, friendly, sturdy, colourful, never gritty or realistic.

Output one image: a turnaround sheet on a pure white background. One row of five views of the same piece, left to right: front, side, back, three-quarter, top. Every view orthographic with no perspective, all at exactly the same scale, the piece standing upright with its handle down (the head, blade or hoop up; a shovel or broom with its blade or straw down) and its lowest point on one shared horizontal baseline, the views evenly spaced with clear white space between them, never touching or overlapping. Front: its working face (the blade's edge, the net's opening, the shovel's scoop) toward the viewer. Side: seen from the side. Back: from behind. Three-quarter: from the front left, slightly above. Top: looking straight down from above. The piece is exactly the same height in the front, side and back views, so the views line up as modelling blueprints. Even, flat, soft lighting from above, no cast shadows, no floor, no reflections. The piece alone: no hands, no character, no props, no text, no labels, no arrows, no dimension lines, no aura, glow effects or particles.
