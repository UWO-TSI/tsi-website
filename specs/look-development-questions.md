# look-development: open questions for David

Each question carries the assumption the work went ahead on. Answer any of them and the lab or the captures follow.

1. **Captures use smooth mode, not the pixel filter.** The shipped default is `pixelated: true` (`web/lib/game/graphicsSettingsStore.ts:16`, DPR 0.5, no FXAA). Every baseline and preset capture has the pixel filter off so material and shadow detail is readable and FXAA (High) is in the path. Tone, contrast and saturation numbers barely move with the filter. Should the final look be judged with the filter on?
2. **"Overcast" is the rain weather.** The game has no overcast state: WMO cloud codes 1-3 map to `clear` (`web/lib/game/islandWeather.ts:23-29`). The overcast baseline frames are `?weather=rain` (sun ×0.45, 60% desaturated sky, rain particles). Should overcast become its own weather state when the look is applied?
3. **Season forced to summer** in every capture (`?season=summer`), so presets compare on the same palette. Today's real date gives an autumn blend.
4. **A preset is the day look.** In the lab it replaces the profile at whatever time is forced; dawn, golden hour, night and weather are derived later (§3-4). Season and weather still layer on top of it (`lookToLight` feeds `withSeason`/`withWeather`).
5. **Sun direction moved to the side in all three directions.** The shipped sun sits behind the camera (34° off the view axis), which hides most cast shadows. The directions light from screen-left and slightly behind the subject so shadows fall toward the camera. This is a composition choice as much as a lighting one; your references will settle it.
6. **No outline effect.** Row 145 says characters do not need outlines, and nothing in the brief asks for them on props. The lab has no outline control. Say so if you want one tried.
7. **Shadow tint and per-class material controls live in the lab rig only** (`web/components/lab/LookRig.tsx` patches the scene's own materials). Promoting them into the game's material code is part of §4 (apply), once a preset is picked.
8. **The sky gradient is screen-space.** The follow camera pitches down 34° with a 48° vertical FOV, so its top ray is 10° below the horizon; the sky that shows is only there because the curved world bends the terrain down. A world-space sky dome would paint ground colours into that band. The gradient runs from the sky-top colour at the top of the frame to the horizon colour at 55% height.
9. **FPS numbers are relative.** Dev build on this M4 Mac mini, uncapped frame rate, other agents' servers and tools running. They rank the presets' cost; they are not a device benchmark. The Light tier target (30 FPS on integrated graphics) is checked on this machine's integrated GPU only.

## Look apply (§5, row 236), 2026-09-27

Answers to 2, 5 and 7 above as applied: overcast is still the rain state (no new weather state); the sun sits side-front on screen-left (below); the shadow tint and per-class materials moved from the lab rig into the game (`web/components/game/LookMaterials.tsx`, mounted in the member island Canvas, which covers the village, home island, ruins and interiors, and in the applicant Canvas).

10. **Tone mapping is Neutral at exposure 1.5, not the picked preset's ACES at 0.9.** Assumption: "sunnier, bright ground at L* 80-85" outranks keeping the curve. ACES could not lift bright ground past L* 80 without washing the grass pale yellow (exposure 1.2: p95 L* 80, grass saturation 0.38; 1.35: p95 82, saturation 0.36, visibly bleached). Neutral 1.5 gives p95 L* 81 with grass saturation 0.44 and the same blue shadows (lit:shadow 3.8). One field in `/lab/look` (Post → Tone mapping `aces`, exposure 1.2) puts ACES back if the ACES frame read better to you.
11. **Highlights are 0.5% of V1 above L* 90, not "a few percent".** Assumption: accept it. Nothing in the village is bright enough: a white diffuse surface in full sun renders at about 0.7 linear, so any curve that pushes it past L* 90 also pushes the grass toward white. Toy gloss up to 0.9 on props and characters changed the share by under 0.1 point (the side-front sun puts its mirror direction away from the camera), and a lower bloom threshold adds glow, not near-white pixels. Getting there needs either brighter albedos on props or a glossier "toy" material pass; say which, if either.
12. **The sun's elevation follows the phase, not the minute.** Real sunrise/sunset still picks dawn, day, golden hour and night (rows 173, 188); each phase has one elevation (dawn 21°, day 44°, golden hour 22°, night moon 40°), and the golden-hour sun is mirrored to screen-right (west). A sun that climbs through the day would re-bake the cached shadow map every few minutes; ask if you want it.
13. **Day sun lowered from 49° to 44°** (and 3.5 → 3.8 intensity, so ground key and the 4:1 ratio are unchanged) to lengthen shadows; it stays 61° off the camera axis on screen-left, as picked.
14. **Interiors keep their own exposure (1.1).** Their key light takes the outdoor sun's colour and now carries most of the light (key 1.5, ambient 0.32, hemisphere 0.36 by day), with AO and bloom on High. Their point lights were tuned at exposure 1, so the outdoor 1.5 would brighten every room by half.
15. **No foliage wrap added.** With the lower fill the shaded sides of oaks and pines read mid-green in every capture, not black; the spec's wrap would add a shader variant for no visible change.
16. **Light-tier blob shadows take the shadow tint** (a dark blue) but stay centred under their object; they are not offset along the sun direction as the High shadows are.
17. **Rain stays readable as weather:** sky desaturated 45%, sun ×0.45, fill ×1.3, shadows 3× softer at half opacity, grade desaturation +0.08. It measures between the old grey rain and a clear day (see `specs/look/applied.md`).

## Glints and reflections (§6, row 237), 2026-09-27

18. **The water's glint path is folded into view.** The fixed follow camera looks away from the sun (§5.2 put it 61° off the camera's back axis so shadows fall into view), so a physically placed glint path is always behind the viewer. Assumption: keep the sun's elevation and screen side and pull the path to 27° off the view axis (`GLINT_FOLD` 0.45 in `waterShader.ts`). It reads right (glare on the sun's side, low over the sea at golden hour) but it is a stylisation; 0 would drop it, 1 puts it back out of frame.
19. **The clubhouse windows are glass by day, no longer lit amber.** They were flat amber panes with a 0.3 day glow; as glass they read cream with a sky sheen, and the warm glow still takes over at dusk and night (`sheet-close`, window rows). Keep, or keep the amber by day?
20. **Flat windows reflect the sky, not the lawn.** Seen from above, a vertical pane physically mirrors the ground; glass flips its reflection upward so it shows the sky. Vertical panes and posts cannot show the sun's highlight with this sun and camera (their mirror direction is 45-50° off it); the crisp highlights land on curved or tilted glass and metal: lamp globe, lamp cap and base, clock dome, the shop's roof windows, mailbox top, blades.
21. **What counts as metal:** the street lamp and park clock bodies (their whole atlas; light paint such as the clock face stays non-metal), the mailbox, the workbench's iron and steel, weapon blades, brass and the revolver drum. Signs, rope-fence posts and benches stay as they are (no metal material to single out). The starter driftwood sword is wood. Held weapons are covered by tests only; no capture has a metal weapon equipped.
22. **Interiors untouched:** furniture glass (museum cases, tank, wall clock) and the display sword are left out of the glass/metal classes until the interiors pass. The outdoor fitting-room mirror and mailbox are in.
23. **Sparkles are sprites with their own flash clock,** 2 per water cell in the map and one per 5 units² of open sea to 90 units, none under the wharf deck (it sits 6 cm above the water). The moon lane at night is kept at a fifth of the day's glint strength. The old `Ocean.tsx` sea (legacy `GameWorld`) keeps its July glints unchanged.
