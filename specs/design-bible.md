# Design bible

The one canon for how the game looks: UI, art and models. On these topics it wins over every other spec. A spec, lane file or asset that disagrees with it gets fixed or archived; it isn't followed. Changes to the bible are recorded as rows in the decision ledger (`specs/game-world-development-plan.md`).

Chapters: **UI** and **Models** are set (2026-10-28, ledger rows 320 and 319). **Art** is being written from the 2026-10-28 codebase review; until it lands, `specs/art-direction-oct17.md` is the art reference. Older UI docs (`specs/polish/gui-sheet.md`, `specs/polish/ui-themes.md`, `specs/cozy-lighting-and-adaptive-ui-2026-09-17.md`, `specs/game-ui-questions.md`, `web/DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`, `web/STYLE.md`) are history wherever they disagree with this file.

## UI

David, 2026-10-28, on the review pages: "i need you to fuckign lock in on all the ui". The game had two live looks (flat in 11 menus, cream paper in about 14 plus the HUD), so every new screen copied whichever one its brief named. From now on there is one look.

**The look**
1. **Clean flat is the one base for the whole game UI, with warmth:** menus, sheets, HUD chrome, toasts, prompts, dialogs. That means one flat light panel ("clean simple 1 box or 2 max"), white item cards, black and white type, an icon rail, and coins and gems top left. No boxes inside boxes, no beige and no glass. (Rows 315 and 285; David 10-22: "this is the basic clean color theme for ui".) It must not feel sterile. David 10-28, on the reward pops: "the ui should be a combination, not so flat but also keep the old vibe but new color". Keep the old kit's soft, rounded, friendly shapes and depth (gentle shadows, a little texture where it helps), in the new palette.
2. **Paper only for things that are paper in the world:** letters, notices, the club goal, HQ notices, a restaurant's menu (David 10-28: "PAPER MENU LIKE REAL RESTAURANCE"). Paper is a material inside the flat system. It is not a second UI.
3. **Minimal and visual.** Less text, more icons and pictures: you should understand a screen "just by looking at it". Show what something does on hover, in a line of plain words. Lighter colours, less bold (David 10-28 on the radar, the monument and the market).
4. **Weight matches importance.** Big moments (a rare catch, a level-up, a reward) are juicy: they pop, spring and fly to their slot. Ambient notices (campfire hour, a resident's letter arriving) are gentle and quiet (David 10-28: "very gentle and not something so important").
5. **One action per view and room to breathe** (David 10-18: "more space and cleaner more intuitive design").
6. **Real items, not text:** item pictures are the real model or its rendered icon, with the pixel filter. No emoji (row 281). Food on a menu is the food model (David 10-28).
7. **Rarity colours:** common grey, uncommon green, rare blue, epic purple, legendary yellow, and divine a purple holographic rainbow (`--gui-holo`). Rarity shows as a thin outline or edge, not a fill (David 10-28). Text on divine is white.
8. **HUD at rest:** the quest line, the minimap and the time with its sun, top right (row 309), plus the player's health, stamina and XP bars (David 10-28: "the user shoud have a health, stamina, and exp bar, and everything should be when exp is filled"; a level-up happens when the XP bar fills). No other movement UI. Esc 1 is a UI view with no dimming; Esc 2 is Settings and Log out.
8b. **HUD layout, the two layers** (David 10-29: "the layer 2 ui isnt finalized and there isnt a slot for the coins and gems"):
   - **Layer 1, at rest:**
     - top right: the quest line, the minimap, the time with its sun;
     - top left: the health and XP bars, plus stamina only while it's below full.
     - Nothing else.
   - **Layer 2, the UI view (Esc 1, no dimming):** everything in layer 1, plus:
     - **the wallet:** coins and gems, the one home for both, in its own slot top right directly under the time;
     - the level badge beside the XP bar;
     - the phone, bottom right (8d), whose apps are the menus. There is no bar along the bottom and no place-name title in this view (David 11-04: "tethos island shouldnt take up screen remove"); the place name only flashes as you arrive somewhere.
   - **When coins or gems change at rest:** the wallet slides into its layer-2 slot for about 2 s, counts, then slides away. Every coin and gem animation (rewards, the tutorial's fly-ins, sales) flies to that slot, never to the quest line.
   - One owner: the HUD layout lives in one component tree (TopCluster and the phone), and features add pieces into named slots there, never their own corners.
8c. **Talk uses the icon library** (David 11-01: "make a icon library that matches words to icons so all future convo uses colored icons"). Every line of talk (residents, the tutorial, quests, letters read aloud) goes through the kit's `Keyword` library (`web/components/gui/flat/keyword.tsx`, `keywordParts`): a word it knows shows in its tone's colour with its little flat icon wiggling before it. Lines are written plain; nobody marks words by hand. A new creature, item, place, role or action that people talk about gets its row in the library (one kind, its icon, its tone, its words), never a one-off icon inside a feature. The tones are the `--ui-word-*` tokens: sea creatures, land creatures, coins, gems (and rewards and events), things, places, people, things you do. The full library is on `/lab/gui?only=keyword-library`.
8d. **The phone** (David 11-04: "pressing a button and a phone pops up on the bottom right and the apps are like all the stuff in the bar so that we dont need the bottom bar anymore"). The kit's `Phone` (`web/components/gui/flat/phone.tsx`, on `/lab/gui?only=phone`) is the one home for the menus; no feature adds a menu bar or a corner of buttons of its own, it adds an app.
   - **The phone itself** (round 10, David 11-04: "phone model, make it 3d blender with a slide up animation"): a chunky slider phone, cream-white with a soft mint tint, modelled in headless Blender (`art/ui/slider_phone.py`) from his reference. The top half carries the backlit pale-green screen, three jelly heart keys, a round d-pad with teardrop soft keys either side, and our own marks (clovers, a flower, hearts, yellow stars; never a licensed character); the bottom half is a 4 by 3 keypad of glowing pill keys, hidden until the top slides up; a braided green strap with a lime jelly star hangs off the left side.
   - **How it's built, and its cost:** three layers rendered from the model with one orthographic camera, turned 20 degrees and not tipped, so the glass is an upright rectangle (the body with the keypad, the sliding top half, the strap and star; `web/public/assets/ui/phone/*.webp`, about 69 KB in all), placed and moved in CSS, with the screen as live HTML laid straight on the rendered glass (`phoneArt.ts`, written by `art/ui/phone_layers.py`). No WebGL: no draw calls, no frame time beyond compositing. A change to the model reruns both scripts.
   - **The pixel filter** (round 11, David 11-04: "the phone also needs a pixel filter"): the phone wears the world's pixel finish at the world's pixel size, `PIXEL_FINISH` (`lib/game/qualityTier.ts`: 2 CSS px a pixel on every tier and screen; the island's canvas renders at 1 / PIXEL_FINISH). Each layer is drawn onto a small canvas, one canvas pixel a world pixel, averaged down with its edge made hard (the world has no antialiasing), and scaled up without smoothing. Every box (the layers, the slide, the strap's pivot, the screen) is rounded to whole world pixels from the phone's width, which CSS keeps a whole number of pixels, so the layers and the screen share one grid. The finish has no colour step of its own (the weather's grade belongs to the 3D scene, and UI isn't graded). With the pixel finish off in the settings the layers draw at the screen's resolution; the screen's pixel art keeps its size, because it is the screen's style.
   - **Where and how big:** the bottom right, 16 px in from the side; the keypad's plain foot below the keys hangs off the bottom edge, as if held up from below. The open phone fits under the top right's HUD (the wallet's slot): the body is about 36% of the window's height less 400 px (300 px on a phone screen), between 150 and 220 px wide (280 on a phone screen, and never wider than the screen less its edges). In a window too short for the 150 px phone it sinks lower instead (more of the keypad below the edge), so its top still clears the wallet. The app tiles grow with it (16 to about 30 pixels).
   - **The screen in pixel art** (round 11, David 11-04: "phone ui reference, change color but keep the pixel-y style", from a DS game's screen: its style only, never its art, font or text). Every phone screen and later phone app follows these rules:
     - **One pixel size:** `PX` (`components/gui/flat/pixel.tsx`, = PIXEL_FINISH). Every size and offset on the screen is a whole number of pixels (`calc(N * var(--px))`), and every picture is drawn one canvas pixel an art pixel and scaled up by PX with `image-rendering: pixelated`, so the pixels stay square at the island's size on a desktop and a phone screen.
     - **Our pixel art, in the phone's colours:** sprites are rows of characters in `pixel.tsx` (o deep green outline, w cream, m mint, d deep mint, y star yellow, a the icon's tone from the keyword library), drawn at runtime in the theme's `--ui-phone-*` tokens. No hex in the art; no orange or brown.
     - **Frames are 9-slices** (`border-image` of a sprite, its slice a whole number of pixels): the screen's frame (a dark line, a cream line inside it, a small curl in each corner) one pixel in from the glass; pills (deep green with a cream rim) for titles and key caps; light buttons (cream in a dark line, mint when hovered). The rendered glass shows through: no panel of its own, no boxes in boxes.
     - **The layout:** a title pill on the frame's top line (the phone's marks: a clover, a heart, a star; no text), the apps in a grid of three, the home button on the frame's bottom line.
     - **Type:** the kit's font (Nunito), small and bold in the deep green, the only thing not on the pixel grid. No pixel font: one readable at this size would need finer pixels than the world's, and the labels must fit their tiles.
   - **An app:** a square tile 9-sliced from the tile sprite (a pixel border, cream, a mint shade bottom right), its 12 by 12 pixel icon (`PixelIcon`; a new app draws one in `PIXEL_ICONS`, in its keyword's tone) in the middle, its name under it, a count (Counter) top right for new things, and its key bottom right on a desktop as a pixel key cap (none on touch). Hovered or picked with the keyboard it turns mint in a star-yellow ring and lifts a pixel; the keyboard's pick also gets the pointing hand at its left, bobbing a pixel (still under reduced motion).
   - **The apps, in order:** Settings, Quests, Bag, Collection, Friends (who's on the island is inside it), Mail. A new menu joins as an app, in the same tile.
   - **Opening it:** the phone key (Y by default, remappable in Settings) brings it up over play and frees the cursor; Y again, Esc or the home bar puts it away. Esc's UI view (Esc 1) brings it up with the fuller HUD. On a touch screen a small phone button top left (where the menu button was) brings up the UI view with the phone.
   - **Slide-up:** it comes up from below the corner with the spring (about 12% overshoot, a slight tilt settling, 420 ms), then the top half slides up to show the keypad with a small settle (360 ms, from 260 ms) and the star swings on its strap. Away, it plays back: the top slides down (180 ms) and the phone drops (220 ms). Nothing else animates. Reduced motion: it fades in already open where it sits, no rise, no slide, no swing.
   - **An app opening, and getting back:** tapping an app opens that app's own sheet; while it's open the phone tucks down out of the way. Closing the sheet (Esc or its close) brings the phone back up as it was, so you're back on its home screen.

9. **Motion:** a spring with about 12% overshoot; hover about 200 ms, select about 400 ms; transform only; a slight drop shadow on hover; off under reduced motion. When a value changes (a stat, a count), animate the change; never jump.
10. **Never show the coin or gem to CAD rate.**

**How it's built**
1. **Tokens only.** Components read the `--ui-*` theme tokens (`web/styles/themes/`) and nothing else. No hex, `rgb()` or font stacks in component CSS or inline styles. A missing token gets added to the theme file first. Themes swap whole, as a "css color sheet change" (David 10-22).
2. **One kit:** `web/components/gui/flat/` plus the shared pieces in `web/components/gui/` (motion, dialogs, `Keycap`, `Tabs`, `Tooltip`, `Progress`, `Counter`). Use a kit piece if one exists. If none exists (a radar chart, a reward track, a time strip), add it to the kit and the showroom first, then use it. No one-off panels, buttons, tags, bars or bubbles inside features.
3. **One showroom:** `/lab/gui` shows every kit piece in the base theme. A screen goes to David only after its pieces look right there.
4. **Review:** every new or changed screen goes to David as before and after shots on the new world, with frame strips for anything animated.

**Defaulted, David can overturn:** cream stops being a base look; its paper textures stay only as the paper material (rule 2), and a cream or ACNH theme could come back later as a paid theme swap. Nunito stays the one typeface. Ink black is the primary colour. The canonical Button, Keycap and Dialogue move from `components/recruit/ui` into `components/gui`. Recruitment keeps its own look, and the applicant island is unchanged.

## Models

David, 2026-10-28: "models should be considered to have a high def version and a low poly version for close up in shop vs from far away, and these models needs to be made properly on blender and not half assed and models should have direction and reference provided before making them".

1. **Direction and references come first.** No model is started until it has:
   - a direction: what it is, where it's seen and the feel;
   - references that David supplied or approved. When he asks a lane to find them online (David 10-28, the one-pieces: "use blender and online for refernce images"), the lane searches headless and shows him the picks before it models anything.
   A lane that's missing either asks the coordinator, and the coordinator asks David. Without both, no model work starts.
2. **Two versions when a model is seen both up close and far away.**
   - **High-def** is for close-ups: the shop's detail view, item previews, the wardrobe and character creator, museum cases, the trophy case, gift and reward cards, and icons rendered from it.
   - **Low-poly** is for the world: on the ground, held, worn by residents, at a distance.
   - Both share one design: the same silhouette, colours and proportions. The low-poly is reduced from the high-def; it isn't drawn separately.
   - A model only ever seen one way gets one version.
   - The game picks by view. `worldModel` in `web/lib/game/world/active.ts` already serves the world copy while close-ups keep theirs. Grow that switch; don't add a second one.
3. **Made properly in Blender.** Built headless (`/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b --factory-startup -P <script>`). Agents model with scripts, so "properly" means the script does real modelling:
   - Primitives (`art/props/propkit.py`, `art/remodel/rkit.py`) are a blockout only. The script joins and merges them into one mesh, then shapes it (bevels, loop cuts, subdivision, proportional moves) until it matches the references.
   - The result has a clean silhouette, sensible topology with no hidden overlapping shells, proper UVs and named materials.
   - It uses the game's scale, pivot and facing.
   - The high-def is that shaped mesh. The low-poly is decimated or retopologised from it.
   - A visible stack of separate shapes or a lumpy maths blob is not a finished model.
   - **Meshy makes the model, Blender does the edits** (David, 2026-10-29: "I NEED YOU TO CONNECT TO MESHY.AI and use meshy as part of your modeling pipeline"; 2026-11-01: "add meshy.ai as apart of your modeling pipeline, and edits are done on blender"). A new model starts as a Meshy generation: `node art/meshy/meshy.mjs image <name> <ref.png>...` from David's references, or `text <name> "<prompt>"` from his direction. The API key is in the macOS keychain as "Meshy API"; never print it.
     - Meshy output never ships raw. `art/meshy/clean.py` takes it to the game's scale, pivot and facing, one merged mesh, sized textures, the high-def GLB and the low-poly reduced from it. The lane's own Blender script then does the rest: materials mapped to the game's look, shape fixes, parts such as a lantern's light, and effects such as auras added in the game.
     - Every edit after the generation is a Blender script on that mesh, David's review notes included. Never regenerate, remesh or retexture in Meshy to make an edit; a new generation is only for a model that is wrong from the start.
     - Each generation spends David's Meshy credits. A lane logs the credits per model and asks the coordinator before going past 10 generations in a round.
     - This is the one generator allowed. Other asset libraries and generators stay off.
4. **Finished before review.** A model goes to David in both versions: the high-def in its close-up view, and the low-poly in the world from the game camera, beside the model it replaces.
5. **One version in the repo.** When a model replaces another, the old file is deleted or moved to `archive/`, and every path that loaded it moves to the new one. No parallel old copies.
6. **Budgets.** The low-poly fits the world's triangle budget (`specs/perf/`). The high-def is sized for one model on screen at a time.
