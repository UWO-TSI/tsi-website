# Natural terrain: questions and the assumptions taken

Build agent, `game/terrain`, 2026-09-30. None of these blocked; each says what was done. Evidence: `specs/evidence/terrain/` (before/after of today's island from the same cameras, the synthetic fixture, the painter).

1. **How far the coast may drift from the painted squares.** Every painted cell centre stays on its own side (a land cell's centre is land, a water cell's is water), so the coast moves up to about half a cell from the painted edge (more at corners, where it rounds). That keeps fishing, water classes, reachability and object checks true to what is drawn, and a one-cell river or spit survives (it draws about 0.8 wide). Letting the coast cross cell centres ("a cell or more") would let the coast and those per-cell systems disagree. Want it looser? It is one constant, `COAST.core`, plus a pass that reclassifies cells from the contour.
2. **How organic.** Open-sea coasts get noise of about ±0.5 cell over a 5–6 cell wavelength on top of the blur's rounding; narrow rivers and the canal get a tenth of that so they keep tidy banks. Stone, wood and brick meet the water crisp (a pier or quay stays straight). Stronger: `COAST.sea`.
3. **Slope vs cliff, as data.** No new flag: one level between neighbours is a slope (blended, walkable), two or more a kit cliff. Hills and mountains are stacks of one-level steps.
4. **Blend width.** Kept your 2026-07-29 call ("the 8 block 8 degree change is a little over kill"): a lone one-level step still blends over about three cells. What changed is that slopes no longer terrace: the height field is a diffusion that only stops at cliffs.
5. **Health, loosened** (tests updated with it): flat ground (any level, a cell whose eight neighbours share its level) at least 60% of land, replacing "level 0 at least 70%"; the 8% half-step cap and the level-4 cap are gone (the painter already caps at 6). Objects count as ashore when the coast is within a body's reach (0.2): today's flower-3 sits exactly on the river bank's painted line at (−11, 1.5), which the organic bank now puts on the lip.
6. **The Slope brush's steepness.** A level per 2.5 cells (about 17°): green, walkable. Stony ground fades in from a rise of 0.33 (a level per ~2.3 cells) and is full by 0.6; raise/lower rings 1.5 cells apart make a rocky mountainside. The rock is the soil surface in a cool grey: the cliff kit's rock texture is strata for a vertical wall and reads as ploughed furrows on a slope. If you have a scree or rock-ground texture in the dump, it drops in here (`ROCK`).
7. **Slope at the coast.** The brush brings a hill back down to sea level at the shore rather than raising coastal cliffs; paint a Cliff for rocks at the water.
8. **Cliff brush base.** A stroke stands one tier above the level where it started (Alt: one below). Painted over lower ground it can leave a face taller than the kit; health reports it and "legalise" fixes it, as before.
9. **Soft land/sea** applies to the round brush and the line tool; rect, fill and lasso paint exact cells.
10. **The beach.** Sand slopes the 0.078u down to the waterline over 1.5 cells and the bed carries on below it; the wet band holds for 0.35 cells and dries by 1.2. Grass banks keep the river's 0.12 edge and the grass drape (which now follows the organic bank). Beach only where sand is painted.
11. **The half step moved half a cell.** The old height field blurred around one cell's centre and stored it on a corner, so every blend sat half a cell toward +x/+z. It is centred on its boundary now; today's Oracle rise shifts by that half cell (walking and the mesh agree, tested).
12. **The preview lost the orange half-step lines**: slopes now read as hill shading, the same as in 3D. Cliff lines stay. Want the lines back as a toggle?
13. **Blend weights live on the vertices**, not in a per-chunk texture: the cells along an edge are already cut to the lattice for the coast, and each surface keeps its own material and shader.
14. **Cost.** Today's island: 299 → 298 draws, 204k → 214k triangles in the plaza view, 144 FPS both (display-capped); terrain derivation 47 ms and mesh 84 ms at load. A 256×256 island: ~0.7 s to build on map change (the game rebuilds only then).
15. **The applicant island** shares GridWorld, so it got the organic coast and blends too. No layout change.
