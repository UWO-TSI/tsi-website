The Titanium, round 3: questions

Grey titanium with the rainbow only at a slant; the awakening unlocks it section by section into a neon beam with its sections floating round it.

  1. The metal. The rod is grey steel now (each hex flat a shade lighter or darker than the next, the seams dark). The thin film is still there but weighted by the viewing angle: almost none on a face turned to you, all of it on faces seen edge-on, so the rainbow runs along the edges and comes and goes as it turns. Stronger or fainter at the edges?
  2. Seven sections, three plates each. The blank is seven sections (was six), each split into three plates (was two), each section's split turned 30 degrees from the one below. Every floating plate has a small fin. The shut rod is one draw in a hand at 594 of 600 triangles, so seven sections is the most that fits the tool budget with the seam bands kept. More would mean dropping the dark seam band round each section. Keep seven?
  3. The unlock. Through the pulse, one section at a time from the bottom: a short shake, a latch's click and a puff of grey smoke from its three splits, then it cracks open a little, angled outwards. At the burst every section floats out round the beam and they start turning, each the other way from the one below. The reel's halves and the claws open at the burst too. Faster, slower, or more smoke?
  4. Awakened, at rest and in use. The sections hover, turn round the beam and hang back on a spring when the rod moves, the higher ones looser, so they follow in a stagger. Cost in a hand: the rod (264 triangles) plus its effect (1,188 triangles: the 21 plates with their fins, the beam, its haze and the rings), 5 draws, the limit for a rod.
  5. The mark. An awakened item's card shows a small round bolt in its top right corner, in its rung's colour (epic purple here). Only the bag's tools shelf shows it today (the only items that awaken are rods; the shop's cards keep their arrow there). Right icon?
