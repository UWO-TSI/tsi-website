# props and enemies art: open questions

Each has the assumption the build uses today. None blocks.

1. **Staff and tome grip on the back.** `Character.tsx` GRIP gives `staff` and `summon` a back rotation of `[0, 0, 2.6]`, which suits the old dump staff (its head pointed down its -Y). The new weapons follow one convention (grip at the origin, tip/head up +Y glTF), so on the back the oak and rune staffs hang head-down by the knees (`weapons_sheet.png`, 4th frame). Swords and bows look right. *Assumption:* the character engine owner changes `staff.back` and `summon.back` to `[0, 0, -0.5]` (the sword's tilt mirrored). That file is outside this task, so I didn't edit it.
2. **Staff in the hand.** The staff shares the sword's hand grip, so in Idle/Run it points forward like a lance, and in AttackCast it lies across the chest before rising. *Assumption:* fine for now. A tuned staff grip is a character-engine change, not a model change.
3. **Bow at rest.** In Idle the bow grip holds the bow flat and pointing outward. It stands upright only in AttackBow, which is the pose it is built for. *Assumption:* same as 2.
4. **Crafted weapons aren't wired.** `sword-iron`, `bow-yew`, `revolver-brass` and `staff-rune` have GLBs at `/assets/game/weapons/<id>.glb` (modelScale 1.3). `WEAPON_LOOK` in `lib/game/combat/data.ts` only lists the four starters. Adding an entry would also make that weapon usable in the ruins with cooldown/range numbers I would have to invent. *Assumption:* the combat owner adds the look rows (and their stats) when crafted gear reaches the loadout.
5. **Other roster weapons.** `shield-buckler`, `totem-cedar` and `wraps-cloth` are tier-1 non-starters that are neither crafted nor higher tier, so they have no model. The buckler would need a second hand socket. *Assumption:* model them when they get looks.
6. **Branch pickup.** Shaking a tree adds the branch straight to your pockets. `VillageLife` renders nothing for `sub: "wood"`, and there's no drop beat. The branch GLB is used as material on the workbench top. *Assumption:* whoever adds a tree-shake drop animation uses `/assets/game/props/branch.glb` (scale 1.3).
7. **Wisp, book and elder crab aren't in the ruins spawns.** `SPAWNS` and `WAVES` only use fox, crab, mushroom, golem and the boss, so the wisp, the book and the elder crab only appear in the contact sheet and `/lab/item`. *Assumption:* spawn placement is the combat owner's call.
8. **Telegraph glow is a slot only.** Every enemy has an emissive material named `telegraph` on its `glow_*` nodes (eyes, core, sigil, halo, rune bands) and on the wisp's ring runes, at full intensity. `EncounterRender` still shows windup as the instance tint and the red ground marker. *Assumption:* ramping `emissiveIntensity` on that material during windup is a later engine pass. The part hierarchy (body → head → glow_eyes, body → arm_l → glow_arm_l) is kept in the GLB for it.
9. **Draw calls.** The instancer makes one InstancedMesh per GLB primitive per enemy type. The five types the ruins spawn come to 59 primitives (fox 12, crab 11, mushroom 7, golem 14, boss 15; `catalog.json` lists each part's materials). The old placeholders used a handful. *Assumption:* acceptable for the ruins scene. If the budget bites, the upgrade is merging each enemy's static parts (legs into the body) until procedural animation needs them.
